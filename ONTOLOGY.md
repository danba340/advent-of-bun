# Ontology

Shared language for Lisa Bark's job-tailored portfolio. Terms only, no process steps.

## People

**Lisa**:
Lisa Bark, the candidate. She runs the chat. Source of truth for anything not already in the sources.
_Avoid_: treating Daniel as a stand-in for her answers.

**Recruiter**:
Lisa Hannerz at Framtiden. First human reader of the application.
_Avoid_: calling her Lisa.

**Assessor**:
The person judging the portfolio for the current job. First the Recruiter, then Husqvarna's design hiring side.

## Sources

**Application**:
`past-applications/application.pdf`. Lisa's April 2026 municipal UX application. Facts in it are trusted. Do not re-ask them.
_Avoid_: CV, the old PDF.

**Job**:
The current target role, written up in `jobs/`. Right now: UX/UI Designer on Husqvarna Fleet, via Framtiden (`jobs/HUSKVARNA-UX-2026.md`).
_Avoid_: Husqvarna as a synonym for the Job. Husqvarna is the company.

**Ascom**:
Lisa's current employer and the main pool of Projects to surface. The Application names some of that work. Goal 1 asks for more.

**Transport**:
Ascom's hospital internal transport system. One product, two of the three Portfolio Projects: Coordinator desktop and Porter mobile.
_Avoid_: treating Transport as a third, separate Project.

**Coordinator desktop**:
Portfolio Project. Lisa was lead designer. Desktop software for the Coordinator. Main structure of the coordination workflow: overview of different types of transports. Dashboard of tasks (time, criticality, overdue). Control of each Porter's workload, breaks, and shifts. Showable Screenshots: the main Dashboard, Administrator overview of Task type settings, Administrator configuration of Task types.

**Dashboard**:
The Coordinator desktop surface where the Coordinator mainly works. Reads tasks, focusing on time, criticality, and overdue statuses.
_Avoid_: calling the whole Coordinator desktop the Dashboard.

**Administrator**:
Desktop user who configures Transport. Sets up Task types. Not the Coordinator on the 8-hour shift.

**Task type**:
A kind of transport. The Administrator configures them. The Coordinator then sees those types in the Dashboard.

**Porter mobile**:
Portfolio Project. Lisa owned it the same way as Coordinator desktop. Mobile workflow for the Porter. Two hospital ways of working: Worklist, or Coordinator priority (mostly 1–2 transports in a row). Acute transports must be clear: sound, vibration, and visuals help the Porter prioritize tasks in the list. Showable Screenshots: Worklist; the page for accepted and Ongoing tasks.

**Acute transport**:
A high-priority transport on Porter mobile. It has to be clear when the Porter receives one. Sound, vibration, and visuals help them prioritize tasks in the list.
_Avoid_: mixing this up with Critical alarm handling, or with criticality on the Coordinator Dashboard.

**Worklist**:
Hospital way of working on Porter mobile. The team collectively receives tasks and picks from a shared list. Also a showable screen.

**Ongoing**:
Porter mobile page for accepted and ongoing tasks.

**Coordinator priority**:
Hospital way of working on Porter mobile. The Porter receives what the Coordinator sets as the priority, mostly one or two transports in a row.
_Avoid_: treating this as the only mobile workflow. Some hospitals use Worklist. No separate Screenshot of this mode has been offered.

**SASICU**:
Portfolio Project. EU research on ICU alarm handling. Lisa was the main UX person. Interviews and shadowing in three hospitals (two in the Netherlands, one in Sweden). Insights. Initial concept for alarm levels. Ongoing. Might become a product or not; that outcome is not shareable. Not a shipped UI Project. Showable Screenshots: pictures of the shadowing, a blurred high-level concept, flows.
_Avoid_: claiming a product result. Do not ask whether it became a product.

**Coordinator**:
Desktop user of Transport. Sits an 8-hour shift in larger hospitals. Reads the Dashboard. Controls each Porter's workload, breaks, and shifts.
_Avoid_: executer. The person who runs the tasks is the Porter.

**Porter**:
Mobile user of Transport. Receives transportation tasks and executes them. Depending on hospital: picks from a Worklist, or follows Coordinator priority.

**Critical alarm handling**:
Ascom product for receiving and viewing alarms of different criticality. Lisa's work on it is evaluation (formative and summative, including icons and a large release), in the medical-device process, not design of this product. Opening evidence, not one of the three.
_Avoid_: heart-monitoring alarms as the Project name.

## Work products

**Project**:
One piece of Lisa's past work that could go in a portfolio. In the Portfolio it uses four blocks: situation (product, users, her role), what she did, what changed, and Screenshots placed where they earn the next sentence.
_Avoid_: case study, tools-used strip, double diamond.

**Portfolio**:
The document we are making for the Job. HTML first, then editable docx and pdf. Written in English, first person. Narrative at Application level of detail, plus screenshots. A short opening, three Projects from Ascom, and a close with contact. No CV dump. Each Project is scannable in about a minute. Opening stays under about 150 words.

**Opening**:
The short first section of the Portfolio. Who Lisa is, what she works on, why this Job.
_Avoid_: about me, bio, CV.

**Close**:
The last section. Contact only.

**Screenshot**:
A still of Lisa's work that she is allowed to include. She adds these to the repo before Goal 3. Not a stock image and not a recreation unless she says it is.

## Flagged ambiguities

**Lisa** means Lisa Bark unless we say Recruiter or use the surname Hannerz.

**Critical alarm handling** is Lisa's name for the receive-and-view alarm product. The Application still says she designs UI for heart-monitoring alarms. Do not treat those as proof she designed this product.
